# Retail Sales Analysis: The Discount Illusion

*Data Source: Kaggle (Author: danielsowah123) - https://www.kaggle.com/datasets/danielsowah123/retail-sales-dataset*

## 🧠 The Starting Question (My Hypothesis)
As an analyst, I don't just look at numbers. I ask questions. Looking at this retail dataset, a common business assumption is: *"If we offer higher discounts, we will sell more and make more money."* 
I decided to test this assumption. Here is the brutal truth the data revealed.

### 1. The Fallacy: Does Discount Increase Sales? (Left Chart)
**The Truth:** The data screams **NO**. As the discount percentage increases from 0% to 25%, the number of orders actually *plummets*. The highest sales volume happens when there is **zero discount**. This suggests the business is heavily reliant on full-price buyers, or the discounted items are simply not attractive.

### 2. The Consequence: The Profit Margin Massacre (Middle Chart)
**The Truth:** If the first chart wasn't bad enough, this one is a disaster. A 25% discount doesn't just reduce revenue; it obliterates the profit margin. The margin crashes from a healthy **25.04%** down to a catastrophic **0.20%**. The business is practically giving products away for free, working just to break even.

### 3. The Culprit: Who is bleeding the cash? (Right Chart)
**The Truth:** To find the root cause, I drilled down into the product categories. The result is a bloodbath. **Laptops** are losing a massive **$45.95k per order** (likely due to aggressive discounts and high return rates). Meanwhile, **Accessories** are the saving grace, generating a steady **$11.44k** profit per order.

---

## 🎯 The Final Verdict (Business Recommendation)
Stop discounting immediately. Specifically, **stop discounting Laptops**. You are bleeding money to sell items that customers don't even want at a discount. Shift your entire marketing focus to high-margin Accessories and maintain full price on top-performing products.
